# Rust baseline (2026-10-24)

- Environment: Intel(R) Xeon(R) Processor @ 2.10GHz (4 cores), Linux-6.18.44-fc-v153-x86_64-with-glibc2.39
- Tools: rustc 1.97.0 (2d8144b78 2026-07-07); Ubuntu clang version 18.1.3 (1ubuntu1); bmb `build` (clang -O2)
- Method: the median (wall clock) of 9 interleaved runs per variant. Measured after the outputs were checked to agree.
- A ratio < 1 means BMB is faster.

| Bench | BMB ms | Rust safe ms | Rust unchecked ms | BMB/safe | BMB/unchecked |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| sieve | 303.2 | 307.4 | 298.6 | 0.986 | 1.016 |
| isort | 131.2 | 151.1 | 144.7 | 0.869 | 0.907 |
| kadane | 175.1 | 171.9 | 162.3 | 1.019 | 1.079 |
| gcdsum | 121.3 | 121.5 | 122.1 | 0.998 | 0.994 |
| hist | 54.0 | 53.6 | 54.7 | 1.009 | 0.988 |
| gather | 102.6 | 103.9 | 107.9 | 0.987 | 0.951 |
| vadd | 189.4 | 194.2 | 261.1 | 0.975 | 0.725 |

## Interpretation

- **The difference between safe Rust and unchecked Rust is at the level of measurement noise in all 7** (only in vadd is unchecked slower, of all things). Bounds checks are nearly free with branch prediction, and in loops LLVM hoists the checks out of the loop and they do not block vectorization either. The same held for indirect indices (hist, gather) and slices of unknown length (vadd).
- So **on these benches no speed gain from "removing checks by proof" is observed.** The difference between BMB and safe Rust was 0.92 to 1.20 in the first measurement. The 20% difference of sieve was because the elements of a `bool` array were kept in memory as `i1`, so LLVM could not use fills and vectorization; after the change to bytes it became 1.03. Now all 7 are within roughly ±5%.
- By this result the gain of proof lies less in speed than in the **absence of failure paths** (no panic and no overflow, proved). The priority of the hypothesis of a performance advantage (DESIGN.md principle 9) is set again on the basis of this result.
